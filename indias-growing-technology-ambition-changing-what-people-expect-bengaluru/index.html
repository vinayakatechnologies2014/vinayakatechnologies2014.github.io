Is India's Growing Technology Ambition Changing What People Expect From Bengaluru?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 4 Aug 2026 - 18:08
Opinion

Bengaluru remains one of India's strongest technology and startup centres, but its technology identity is increasingly moving beyond traditional IT services. The 2026 Global Startup Ecosystem Report ranked Bengaluru 15th globally, with its startup ecosystem valued at about $152.8 billion, making it the third-largest startup ecosystem in Asia.

Karnataka is also pushing Bengaluru towards newer areas such as artificial intelligence, DeepTech, robotics and advanced research. The state has announced initiatives including an AI University, the Bengaluru Robotics and AI Innovation Zone, and Innoverse, a DeepTech initiative intended to strengthen the technology and startup ecosystem.

The state's AI push is not limited to announcements. In January 2026, Karnataka approved the Centre for Applied AI for Tech Solutions (CATS) at the KEONICS facility in HSR Layout, Bengaluru, in partnership with NASSCOM. The centre is intended to support AI, robotics, automation, digital transformation and DeepTech startups, with a reported outlay of ₹20 crore over four years.

Bengaluru's technology growth is also becoming more closely connected with universities and industry. CHRIST University and IBM recently launched an AI Innovation Center in Bengaluru, focusing on AI research, education and talent development, including areas such as generative AI, large language models, agentic AI and responsible AI.

However, Bengaluru's growing technology ambitions raise a broader question: can the city's infrastructure keep pace with the ecosystem it is building? The city continues to attract companies, startups and skilled professionals, while transport, housing and other urban services remain important concerns. This makes Bengaluru's future a question not only of technology leadership, but also of how effectively the city can support the people and businesses driving that growth.

Another significant development is Karnataka's attempt to reduce the concentration of technology activity in Bengaluru. The state's Beyond Bengaluru programme is focused on developing technology clusters in cities including Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi, Kalaburagi, Shivamogga and Tumakuru, with different locations being encouraged to develop their own technology strengths.

This decentralisation could ultimately benefit Bengaluru as well. Expanding technology opportunities beyond the capital could reduce some of the pressure created by the concentration of jobs, investment and talent, while allowing other Karnataka cities to develop specialised ecosystems of their own.

The upcoming Bengaluru Tech Summit 2026 further reflects this change in direction. The 29th edition will be held from November 17 to 19 at the Bangalore International Exhibition Centre under the theme "AI & Beyond", with the programme covering AI, DeepTech and other emerging technologies.

In my view, India's growing technology ambition is changing what people should expect from Bengaluru. The city can no longer be judged only by the number of technology companies or startups it attracts; its ability to provide strong research, talent, infrastructure and a sustainable urban environment will increasingly determine whether it can remain competitive as India's technology ambitions move into AI and DeepTech.
