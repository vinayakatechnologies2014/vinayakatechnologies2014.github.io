India Attends Afghanistan’s 'Victory Day' Event in Delhi: What Has Changed in New Delhi’s Taliban Engagement?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 18 Aug 2026 - 10:34
Verified

India participated in a diplomatic event in New Delhi marking the fifth anniversary of the Taliban's return to power in Afghanistan.

The event was held at the Afghan Embassy in New Delhi on August 17, 2026. M. Anand Prakash, Joint Secretary handling the Pakistan-Afghanistan-Iran division in India's Ministry of External Affairs, attended the reception as chief guest.

The event marked August 15, 2021, when the Taliban entered Kabul and returned to power following the withdrawal of US-led international forces from Afghanistan.

India has not formally recognised the Taliban government as the government of Afghanistan.

The attendance of an Indian senior foreign ministry official at the event is nevertheless a confirmed development in India's diplomatic engagement with the authorities in Kabul.

What happened in New Delhi?

The Afghan Embassy in New Delhi hosted a reception on August 17 to mark the fifth anniversary of what the Taliban calls "Victory Day."

The anniversary is observed on August 15 and commemorates the Taliban's return to Kabul in 2021.

Anand Prakash, Joint Secretary of the Pakistan-Afghanistan-Iran division of India's Ministry of External Affairs, attended the event.

Afghanistan's Chargé d'Affaires in New Delhi, Mufti Noor Ahmad Noor, addressed the gathering.

Diplomats and representatives from several countries also attended the reception.

The event was significant because it publicly brought together representatives of the Taliban-run Afghan diplomatic mission and an Indian senior foreign ministry official.

What is India's current position?

India has not formally recognised the Taliban administration.

At the same time, India has maintained diplomatic and practical engagement with Afghanistan since the Taliban returned to power in 2021.

India's approach has included humanitarian assistance, diplomatic communication and the maintenance of an Indian diplomatic presence in Kabul.

The latest event does not constitute a formal announcement of recognition.

There has also been no announcement establishing that India has entered into a formal alliance with the Taliban.

India's diplomatic engagement with Afghanistan

India's diplomatic presence in Afghanistan changed after the Taliban took control of Kabul in August 2021.

Indian diplomatic personnel were evacuated from Afghanistan following the Taliban takeover.

In June 2022, India returned a technical team to Kabul to coordinate humanitarian assistance and maintain diplomatic communication.

India subsequently increased its engagement with Kabul.

In October 2025, Afghan Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi visited India.

The visit was followed by further diplomatic and economic contacts between India and Afghanistan.

India also upgraded its diplomatic presence in Kabul.

These developments occurred before the August 2026 Victory Day reception.

Who is Anand Prakash?

Anand Prakash is a senior Indian foreign ministry official.

He serves as Joint Secretary of the Pakistan-Afghanistan-Iran division in India's Ministry of External Affairs.

His attendance at the August 17 reception was therefore an official-level diplomatic presence.

The attendance does not, by itself, establish that India has recognised the Taliban government.

What did the Afghan representative say?

Afghanistan's Chargé d'Affaires, Mufti Noor Ahmad Noor, spoke about Afghanistan's relationship with neighbouring and regional countries.

He described Afghanistan's geographical position as an opportunity for connectivity, cooperation and economic development.

He also spoke about regional trade, investment, transit, energy, infrastructure and economic cooperation.

Regarding India, Noor called for stronger political trust, economic partnership and continued engagement between Afghanistan and India.

He said stronger relations could contribute to regional stability, connectivity and economic cooperation.

Why August 15 is significant

August 15, 2021, is the date associated with the Taliban's return to power in Kabul.

The Taliban entered Kabul after the collapse of the previous Afghan government.

The United States and its allies subsequently completed their military withdrawal from Afghanistan.

The Taliban refers to August 15 as "Victory Day."

The August 17 event in New Delhi was organised to mark the fifth anniversary of that event.

India and Afghanistan after 2021

India has continued humanitarian assistance to Afghanistan following the Taliban takeover.

Indian assistance has included humanitarian supplies and other forms of support.

India has also maintained diplomatic communication with Kabul.

The relationship has gradually expanded beyond humanitarian engagement to include political, economic and trade discussions.

The visits of Afghan officials to India have also increased.

The October 2025 visit of Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi was an important diplomatic contact between the two sides.

Further engagement has taken place in areas including trade, economic cooperation and regional connectivity.

Does the latest event mean India recognises the Taliban?

No.

India has not formally recognised the Taliban government.

The presence of an Indian foreign ministry official at an Afghan diplomatic reception is a diplomatic engagement, but it is not equivalent to a formal recognition announcement.

No recognition announcement was made at the August 17 event.

The event therefore does not change India's formal position on recognition.

Has India's engagement with Kabul increased?

Yes.

India's practical and diplomatic engagement with Kabul has increased since the initial withdrawal of Indian diplomatic personnel in 2021.

The sequence of developments includes:

  • India evacuated its diplomatic personnel from Afghanistan after the Taliban takeover in August 2021.
  • India sent a technical team to Kabul in June 2022.
  • Afghan and Indian officials subsequently maintained diplomatic communication.
  • Afghan Foreign Minister Amir Khan Muttaqi visited India in October 2025.
  • India's diplomatic presence in Kabul was subsequently upgraded.
  • A senior Indian foreign ministry official attended the Afghan Embassy's Victory Day reception in New Delhi in August 2026.

These developments establish an increase in direct engagement between India and the authorities governing Afghanistan.

What remains unchanged?

India has not formally recognised the Taliban government.

The August 17 reception did not include an announcement of formal recognition.

India's participation in the event also did not establish a military alliance or political alliance between India and the Taliban.

The event was a diplomatic reception hosted by the Afghan Embassy in New Delhi.

Why the development matters

The August 17 event is significant because it demonstrates a higher level of visible diplomatic interaction between India and Taliban-ruled Afghanistan.

A senior Indian foreign ministry official attended an event organised by the Afghan diplomatic mission to mark the Taliban's return to power.

The Afghan side also publicly called for stronger political and economic relations with India.

At the same time, India's formal position on recognition remains unchanged.

The development therefore establishes closer engagement without formal recognition.

Verified Conclusion

India's participation in the Afghan Embassy's August 17, 2026 reception is a confirmed sign of continued diplomatic engagement with Taliban-ruled Afghanistan.

However, the event did not amount to India's formal recognition of the Taliban government.

India's relationship with Kabul has developed gradually since 2021, moving from limited contact and humanitarian assistance toward broader diplomatic, economic and political engagement.

The latest development therefore establishes one clear fact:

India is maintaining and expanding engagement with the authorities in Kabul while continuing not to formally recognise the Taliban government.
