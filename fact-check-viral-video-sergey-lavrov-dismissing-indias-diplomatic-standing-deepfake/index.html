Fact Check: Viral Video of Sergey Lavrov Dismissing India's Diplomatic Standing Is a Deepfake

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sat, 7 Nov 2026 - 10:29
Fact Checked

A video circulating on social media, purportedly showing Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov dismissing India's diplomatic standing, has been found to be digitally manipulated. The clip makes it appear as though Lavrov described India as merely an oil-trading partner and said that New Delhi had no right to interfere in Russia's foreign policy. However, he never made these statements.

The viral video features Lavrov speaking at what appears to be a press briefing. In the clip, he allegedly says that Russia supplies oil to India in exchange for payment and that this commercial relationship does not give India the right to advise Moscow on matters involving Russia, Ukraine, the United States or other countries.

The video was shared across social media platforms, with posts suggesting that Russia was drawing a clear line in its relationship with India. The claims attracted attention because India and Russia have maintained longstanding diplomatic, defence and economic relations.

A closer examination reveals that the video uses genuine footage of Lavrov but replaces the original audio with fabricated speech. The original recording was uploaded by the M World channel on October 31, 2026. It shows Lavrov responding to questions about reports that Russian President Vladimir Putin had refused to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

In the original footage, Lavrov discusses the possibility of a meeting between the two leaders and Russia's position on negotiations. There is no discussion about India being merely a trading partner, nor does he make the alleged comments questioning India's diplomatic role.

A comparison of the original and viral versions reveals that the background, clothing, gestures and movements are almost identical. However, the audio is different, and Lavrov's lip movements in the manipulated version appear unnatural and out of sync with the words being heard.

The Russian Embassy in India also rejected the viral video. Its official account on X responded to one of the posts circulating the clip, warning users against sharing AI-generated fakes.

India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) also issued a clarification through its official fact-check account, describing the video as fake and cautioning people against misleading content.

Fact-check verdict: FALSE. The viral video does not show Sergey Lavrov making the statements attributed to him. It uses authentic footage from a briefing about Russia–Ukraine relations, with fabricated audio added to make it appear that he was criticising India's diplomatic standing.

The incident highlights how AI-generated audio and manipulated videos can turn genuine footage of public figures into misleading political content. Such videos can appear convincing because the person, setting and recording are real, even though the statements being attributed to them are not.

For viewers, the important lesson is to verify the original recording before accepting or sharing a dramatic statement attributed to a political leader or government official. A familiar face and a genuine press conference setting do not guarantee that the words heard in a viral clip are authentic.
