Sowjanya Rape-Murder Case: Fresh SIT Begins Investigation, Reviews Old Case Files in Mangaluru

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sun, 4 Oct 2026 - 10:03
Developing

The fresh Special Investigation Team (SIT) constituted to reinvestigate the 2012 rape and murder of 17-year-old Sowjanya has now formally begun its work, nearly 14 years after the crime.

The development comes after the Supreme Court directed a de novo investigation into the case on September 21 and ordered the Karnataka Government to constitute a new SIT. The Court also set a three-month timeline for the fresh investigation.

Buzz had earlier reported on the Supreme Court's order and the circumstances behind the fresh investigation. The Karnataka Government subsequently constituted the five-member SIT on September 24.

The new team has now moved from formation to actual investigation.

The SIT held its first meeting in Mangaluru on October 2, where officers reviewed the earlier case files and investigation material and discussed the approach for the fresh probe. Responsibilities were also assigned to members of the team.

An investigation office has been arranged in Mangaluru, while SIT officers have also inspected arrangements in Belthangady, where the original case was registered.

The government has additionally assigned 16 police personnel to assist the SIT in the investigation.

The composition of the team had itself become a point of concern for Sowjanya's family. As Buzz had reported earlier, Kusumavati Gowda's legal team had urged the Karnataka Home Department to reconstitute the SIT, raising objections over the inclusion of certain officers.

Additional SP Venkatesh Prasanna, who was part of the newly constituted team, subsequently sought to be relieved from the SIT after concerns were raised about his inclusion. He was not present at the team's first meeting.

The fresh investigation is therefore now underway, but there is no confirmed SIT finding yet of a new suspect, new arrest or new evidence.

At this stage, the team has begun by examining the existing investigation and case records before proceeding with further investigative steps.

The three-month timeline ordered by the Supreme Court now puts the focus on what the new SIT is able to establish through the fresh investigation.
