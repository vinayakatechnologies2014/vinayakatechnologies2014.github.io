Does Karnataka Need a Second Bengaluru — or Stronger Cities?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Thu, 3 Sep 2026 - 11:27
Opinion

The suggestion to develop a second Bengaluru raises a bigger question for Karnataka: does the state really need another Bengaluru, or does it need several strong cities?

NITI Aayog Vice Chairman Dr. Ashok Kumar Lahiri has suggested that Karnataka should seriously consider developing a second Bengaluru to reduce pressure on the state capital.

Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar has said the state will prepare a comprehensive plan based on the suggestion.

The idea makes sense at one level.

Bengaluru has become the centre of Karnataka's technology, startup and IT services economy. The city continues to attract companies, professionals and people from across India. That growth has also increased pressure on infrastructure and the city's ability to accommodate further expansion.

But creating another Bengaluru may not necessarily be the best answer.

Karnataka Already Has Several Cities

Karnataka does not have to start from scratch.

The state already has cities such as Mysuru, Mangaluru, Hubballi-Dharwad, Belagavi, Kalaburagi, Shivamogga and Tumakuru.

The government is already working through the Beyond Bengaluru initiative to strengthen technology, innovation and economic activity outside Bengaluru.

Instead of trying to create one city that becomes another Bengaluru, Karnataka could focus on making several cities strong enough to attract companies, jobs and investment.

One Bengaluru or Many Strong Cities?

A single second Bengaluru could eventually create the same problems that the existing city faces.

If companies, workers and investment become concentrated in one new location, that city could eventually face the same challenges involving traffic, housing, infrastructure and population growth.

A different approach would be to develop multiple cities, with each building strengths in different sectors.

One city could become a technology centre. Another could focus on manufacturing. Another could develop as a logistics or education hub. Coastal cities could strengthen sectors connected to ports, tourism and services.

This could distribute economic growth more evenly across Karnataka.

The Real Test

The success of the idea should not be measured by whether Karnataka creates a city that can be called the "second Bengaluru."

The better question is whether people can find good jobs, businesses can grow and investment can happen without everyone having to move to Bengaluru.

If Karnataka can create several cities with good infrastructure, skilled workers, reliable connectivity and strong economic opportunities, the state may not need another Bengaluru at all.

It may need something better:

A Karnataka where Bengaluru is one major economic centre among several.
