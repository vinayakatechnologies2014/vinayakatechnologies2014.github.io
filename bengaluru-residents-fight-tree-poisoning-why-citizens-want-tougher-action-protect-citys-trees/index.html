Bengaluru Residents Fight Tree Poisoning: Why Citizens Want Tougher Action to Protect the City’s Trees

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 29 Sep 2026 - 12:30
Public Buzz

Bengaluru residents are stepping up their fight against the deliberate damaging of trees after a tree-poisoning incident in Vijayanagar triggered fresh protests and demands for tougher punishment.

On September 28, 2026, residents and environmental activists announced a protest at Freedom Park, seeking stronger action against people who deliberately damage or destroy trees. They want the Karnataka Preservation of Trees Act, 1976 strengthened, including tougher fines and longer jail terms for deliberate damage to trees.

The immediate case involves a tree on 7th Main Road, 3rd Cross Road, Vijayanagar, where holes were allegedly drilled into the trunk and chemicals put inside. The tree began turning yellow and losing leaves. An FIR has been registered against three local residents in connection with the alleged poisoning, according to the Greater Bengaluru Authority's forest officials.

The incident was reported to forest officials on September 11. The tree was then treated to remove the suspected chemicals, and environmental conservationist Vijay Nishanth, popularly known as the “Tree Doctor”, has been trying to save it. He said the tree currently has about a 50% chance of survival, with its condition expected to become clearer over the next one or two months.

But citizens say the concern goes beyond one tree. Activists have reported complaints involving trees in Vijayanagar, Jayanagar, Kengeri, Indiranagar, Malleshwaram and Byappanahalli, including allegations of poisoning, excessive concretisation and improper cutting. A citizen group said it had received nearly 20 complaints over a five- to six-day period earlier this month.

Residents say the problem is that existing protections do not provide enough deterrence against deliberate damage. They are now demanding stronger enforcement and changes to the tree-protection law so that damaging a city tree carries serious consequences.

For Bengaluru, the issue is bigger than the survival of one tree. Citizens are asking whether the city's remaining roadside trees are being adequately protected when construction, parking, commercial visibility or other private interests come into conflict with them.

The latest protest is therefore an attempt to turn several individual complaints into a wider citizen campaign for stronger protection and enforcement for Bengaluru's urban trees.
