KPSC Scam: Court Calls It “Systematic Fraud” — How Deep Does Karnataka’s Recruitment Network Go?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 9 Sep 2026 - 09:08
Verified

The Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC) recruitment scandal has moved far beyond a suspected question-paper leak. A Karnataka High Court order refusing anticipatory bail to suspended KPSC chairman Sahukar Shivashankarappa has brought fresh attention to allegations of an organised network involving officials, a middleman and candidates.

The case concerns the recruitment of 400 veterinary officers. According to the CID investigation presented before the court, candidates allegedly paid as much as ₹80 lakh to obtain access to examination material and secure selection.

Investigators have alleged that the operation involved more than simply obtaining a leaked paper. Candidates were allegedly given access to the material before the examination and were taken to locations where they could be coached using the leaked questions. The investigation has also examined communications between senior KPSC officials, the alleged middleman and candidates.

The CID has identified 29 candidates among the selected candidates as allegedly connected to the manipulation. The investigation has also involved examination officials and a middleman, and authorities have seized bank accounts while looking for other suspects.

The High Court's concern was not limited to the individual accused. It highlighted the wider damage caused when recruitment examinations are manipulated, particularly because thousands of candidates depend on these examinations for government jobs.

The case is therefore raising a bigger question for Karnataka:

Was this an isolated recruitment fraud, or does it reveal weaknesses in the way government examinations are controlled and monitored?

That question cannot yet be answered conclusively. The allegations are still being investigated and the accused have not been convicted.

But the investigation has already exposed several points that matter: access to examination material, communication between people connected to the recruitment process, alleged payments, candidate selection patterns and the role of intermediaries.

The suspended KPSC chairman is now facing the court process along with other accused persons. The investigation will have to establish who leaked the material, who arranged access, who paid money and whether anyone inside the recruitment system deliberately enabled the process.

For candidates who prepare for years for government examinations, the issue is bigger than one recruitment cycle. If examination integrity can be compromised through an organised network, the damage extends to every genuine candidate who competes for the same job.

The case is still developing. What ultimately matters is whether the investigation can establish the full chain from the alleged leak to the money trail and the appointments.
