H-1B Families Leaving the US: Has the Risk Become More Real Since the Viral Claims?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sat, 10 Oct 2026 - 07:30
Developing

A viral claim in September 2026 suggested that more than 10 H-1B families in the Celina, Melissa and surrounding Dallas areas were returning to India after losing their jobs, allegedly leaving homes, cars and other belongings behind. As explored in our earlier report, H-1B Families Are Allegedly Leaving US Homes and Cars Behind. What Is Actually Happening?, the claim remains unverified, and there is no established evidence of a widespread exodus.

Since that report, developments in US immigration policy have added to the uncertainty facing some Indian professionals and their families.

On September 11, the US Department of Homeland Security proposed eliminating the discretionary 60-day grace period available to certain nonimmigrant workers, including H-1B holders, after their employment ends. The existing grace period gives eligible workers time to seek another employer, explore a change of immigration status or prepare to leave the country. The proposal would remove that provision if finalised. It is important to note that a proposal is not a final rule.

Another development came on October 8, when the US suspended eight major technology companies from the Permanent Labour Certification (PERM) programme, an important step in employer-sponsored green-card applications. The affected companies include Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), Wipro, HCLTech, Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant and Capgemini.

The suspension could disrupt permanent-residency plans for some employees of these companies. However, it does not automatically cancel existing H-1B visas or require affected workers to leave the United States.

For families dependent on one person's employment, losing a job can create immediate financial pressure. Mortgage payments, car loans, children's education and everyday expenses may become difficult to manage while the worker searches for another position. Immigration uncertainty can add to that pressure, particularly for families who have spent years building their lives in the United States or are waiting for permanent residency.

However, the consequences vary by individual circumstances. Not every H-1B worker is affected by the PERM suspension, and not every worker who loses a job must immediately leave the country.

The latest policy developments make the concerns raised by the original viral claim worth examining, but they do not prove that the reported families abandoned their homes or that large numbers of H-1B families are returning to India. Establishing a wider trend would require independently verified cases, credible reporting or reliable data on departures and employment-related immigration changes.

For now, the distinction remains important: the immigration uncertainty is real, but a mass departure of H-1B families has not been established. The question is whether further policy changes and continuing job losses will lead to more documented cases of families choosing to leave the United States.
