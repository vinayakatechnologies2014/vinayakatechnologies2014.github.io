Indiranagar’s Footpath Went Viral. Then the Civic Action Came.

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 6 Oct 2026 - 09:41
Public Buzz

A broken footpath on 12th Main Road in Indiranagar became a much bigger Bengaluru conversation after Canadian vlogger Caleb Friesen posted a video showing damaged slabs, rubble, garbage and large holes along the busy stretch.

The video quickly spread online, drawing millions of views and renewed attention to a problem Bengaluru pedestrians have dealt with for years: basic footpaths that are broken, obstructed or simply unusable.

And then something happened that made the story more interesting.

The Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) moved in.

Workers repaired several of the damaged sections highlighted in the video, cleared debris and addressed some of the most obvious hazards. GBA also publicly shared before-and-after visuals of the work. Friesen later acknowledged the response, saying the footpath was now functionally better, although the work was only temporary.

The Karnataka Lokayukta also took suo motu cognisance of the issue and sent officials to inspect the location. The inspection found construction debris that had reportedly been left unattended on the footpath.

There is an important catch.

The repairs are not the final redevelopment of 12th Main. Friesen said the repaired sections are expected to be dug up again when a larger road revamp is taken up in 2027, with the redevelopment planned to follow Tender SURE standards.

That leaves Bengaluru with a question bigger than one repaired footpath.

Why did this particular footpath receive such rapid attention only after a video went viral?

The issue is not whether social media can help identify civic problems. It clearly can.

The bigger question is whether Bengaluru's civic system can identify and fix such problems before someone has to make them viral.
