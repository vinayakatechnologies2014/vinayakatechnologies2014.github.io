Supreme Court Signals Fresh Probe Into Sowjanya Case, Questions CBI Investigation

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 22 Jul 2026 - 11:55
Developing

The 2012 Dharmasthala Sowjanya rape and murder case has once again come under intense judicial scrutiny, with the Supreme Court examining serious questions surrounding the earlier investigation and considering the possibility of a fresh probe. The development has renewed hopes among Sowjanya's family that the long-pending question of who was responsible for her death could finally be investigated afresh.

A Division Bench comprising Justices Sanjay Kumar and Sanjeev Sachdeva is hearing the petition seeking reinvestigation into the case. The proceedings have focused on the manner in which the investigation was conducted by the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and on whether important aspects of the case were adequately examined.

Questions have also been raised before the court regarding the investigation of the crime scene and the handling of evidence. The proceedings have brought renewed attention to the investigative gaps that emerged during the earlier stages of the case and the failure of the prosecution to establish the guilt of the person who was eventually charged.

At the same time, the Supreme Court's detailed written order on the fresh investigation remains crucial. The order is expected to clarify whether a Special Investigation Team or another investigative mechanism will be entrusted with the matter and what the scope of any fresh probe will be.

What Happened to Sowjanya?

Sowjanya was a 17-year-old second-year Pre-University student studying at SDM College in Ujire, Dakshina Kannada. On October 9, 2012, she did not return home after leaving college. A missing-person complaint was registered at Belthangady Police Station.

The following day, on October 10, her body was found in bushes near Mannasanka. According to the Karnataka High Court's record of the case, the post-mortem examination indicated that she had been raped and strangled. The incident triggered widespread public concern and pressure for a more detailed investigation.

The investigation initially remained with the local police but was later transferred to the Criminal Investigation Department (CID). The CID filed a chargesheet against Santhosh Rao, alleging his involvement in the crime.

However, Sowjanya's parents and others questioned the investigation and maintained that Rao was not responsible for the crime. The Karnataka High Court judgment records that the family and members of the public had raised allegations that other persons should have been investigated. Those allegations were not established as facts by the court.

Amid mounting public pressure, the Karnataka government ordered the transfer of the investigation to the CBI in November 2013. The CBI also filed a chargesheet against Santhosh Rao.

Santhosh Rao Was Acquitted

The case eventually went to trial before the Special CBI Court in Bengaluru. On June 16, 2023, the court acquitted Santhosh Rao.

The Karnataka High Court subsequently recorded that the trial court had found that Rao's involvement in the crime was not established and had also directed the Acquittal Committee to initiate action concerning investigative lapses.

The CBI challenged the acquittal before the Karnataka High Court. The High Court proceedings also considered petitions seeking a fresh or de novo investigation into the crime.

In its August 30, 2024 order, the High Court examined the question of whether a reinvestigation could be ordered. It noted that the case had no eyewitnesses and that the prosecution depended substantially on circumstantial, medical and forensic evidence. The court ultimately dismissed the petitions seeking reinvestigation at that stage, observing that a fresh investigation could not be ordered merely because it was requested and that the circumstances of the case had to be carefully considered.

The High Court also noted an important legal principle: if a fresh investigation were ever ordered, its purpose would be to find the actual perpetrator and not to target a particular individual in advance.

Why Is a Fresh Investigation Being Considered?

The case is now more than 13 years old, making the question of available evidence particularly important. The High Court had previously observed that there were no eyewitnesses and that medical and forensic evidence could not simply be improved with the passage of time.

At the same time, the renewed proceedings before the Supreme Court have brought attention to whether existing material can be reassessed using present-day investigative and forensic capabilities.

Any fresh examination of biological or forensic material would depend on whether the material has been properly preserved, whether its chain of custody can be established and whether usable evidence remains. The possibility of new forensic findings should not be treated as an established result at this stage.

The question before the Supreme Court is therefore not simply whether another investigation should be conducted, but whether such an investigation can meaningfully address the gaps that emerged during the earlier proceedings.

What Happens Next?

The Supreme Court's decision could determine the next chapter of the case. If the court orders a fresh investigation, its directions are expected to specify which agency will conduct the probe, what material can be examined and what safeguards will apply.

A Special Investigation Team is one possible mechanism, but the final decision rests with the Supreme Court.

Any fresh investigation would have to revisit a case that began in 2012. Investigators could potentially be required to examine the original case records, witness statements, forensic material, crime-scene evidence and investigative leads considered during the earlier probes.

The purpose would not be to presume the guilt of any individual. It would be to determine, on the basis of legally admissible evidence, what happened to Sowjanya and whether the available evidence can identify the person or persons responsible for her death.

For Sowjanya's family, the Supreme Court proceedings have reopened a legal battle that has continued for years. The case moved from the local police to the CID and subsequently to the CBI, but the acquittal of the accused left the central question unresolved.

The Supreme Court's intervention could therefore become an important turning point. Whether a fresh investigation can produce meaningful evidence after so many years remains uncertain, but the proceedings have once again placed the Sowjanya case and the questions surrounding its investigation before the country's highest court.
