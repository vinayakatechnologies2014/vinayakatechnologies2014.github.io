Ajit Doval in Beijing: The NSA at the Centre of India-China’s Latest Push for Stability

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Thu, 27 Aug 2026 - 10:48
Verified

National Security Adviser Ajit Doval was in Beijing on August 24–25, 2026, for the 25th round of talks between the Special Representatives of India and China on the boundary question.

The talks placed Doval at the centre of India's latest high-level engagement with China. As India's National Security Adviser, Doval is responsible for advising the Prime Minister on national security matters. He is also India's Special Representative for boundary negotiations and strategic consultations with China.

The August 25 meeting was held with Wang Yi, China's Special Representative and Foreign Minister. The two sides reviewed the boundary question as well as issues connected with maintaining peace and tranquillity along the India-China border.

Why Ajit Doval's Role Matters

Doval's involvement in the India-China boundary dialogue is not new. The Special Representatives mechanism has been an established channel for dealing with the boundary question for more than two decades.

The Government of India appointed Doval as its Special Representative for boundary negotiations and strategic consultations with China as part of his responsibilities as National Security Adviser.

He has subsequently represented India in several rounds of the Special Representatives dialogue. Government records show that Doval led the Indian side in the 19th round of talks in China in April 2016.

The mechanism therefore gives Doval a role that goes beyond routine diplomatic engagement. His mandate covers one of the most sensitive issues in India-China relations: the unresolved boundary question.

What Happened in Beijing

The 25th round of talks took place on August 25, 2026, following Doval's visit to Beijing from August 24 to 25.

According to the Ministry of External Affairs, the two Special Representatives co-chaired the talks and discussed the boundary question as well as broader matters concerning the relationship.

The meeting produced an eight-point set of outcomes and consensus.

Among the key points were the commitment to maintain peace and tranquillity in the border areas, continue efforts related to the boundary question and advance cooperation in areas that affect the populations living along the border. The two sides also agreed to continue working towards a political settlement of the boundary question while keeping their broader interests in view.

This gives the latest Doval-Wang meeting a more concrete significance than simply another diplomatic exchange. Both sides recorded specific areas in which they intend to continue engagement.

The Modi-Xi Direction

Doval's August visit also follows a period of renewed high-level engagement between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

The Government of India has described the 2024 leader-level engagement in Kazan as an important point in the subsequent improvement of India-China relations. In August 2025, India and China also discussed the implementation of the consensus reached by their leaders in Kazan.

Modi and Xi subsequently met in Tianjin on August 31, 2025. Their meeting included discussions on bilateral relations and the importance of maintaining peace and tranquillity in the border areas.

Against that background, Doval's 2026 talks represent the security and boundary channel through which the broader political direction between the two countries is being followed up.

From Leadership-Level Engagement to Boundary Negotiations

The significance of Doval's role lies partly in the connection between the political and security tracks.

Meetings between Modi and Xi establish the broader direction for India-China relations. The Special Representatives mechanism, meanwhile, deals with the much more complicated boundary question.

Doval therefore occupies an important position between India's national-security establishment and its diplomatic engagement with China.

The August 2026 discussions show that maintaining stability along the border remains a central part of India's approach to improving bilateral relations.

What Was Agreed in the Latest Round

The August 25 discussions resulted in eight recorded outcomes and areas of consensus.

The two sides agreed to continue efforts to maintain peace and tranquillity in the border areas and to strengthen mechanisms connected with the boundary question.

They also agreed to continue work towards a political settlement of the boundary question, while pursuing practical cooperation in areas including cross-border exchanges and issues affecting border communities.

The outcomes also provide a framework for continued engagement between the two Special Representatives.

For Doval, this means continuing a diplomatic responsibility that he has held alongside his position as National Security Adviser.

Why This Matters for India-China Relations

The August 2026 talks do not represent the conclusion of the boundary negotiations. Instead, they mark another step in an established process in which India and China are attempting to manage the boundary issue while keeping wider bilateral engagement moving.

The Government of India's latest position places particular importance on peace and tranquillity along the border as an essential condition for stable relations.

Doval's Beijing visit therefore matters for two reasons: it keeps the Special Representatives channel active, and it provides a direct mechanism for India to address the boundary question at a senior strategic level.

For now, the clearest verified development is that India and China have continued high-level engagement on the boundary question and have recorded specific areas of consensus following the 25th round of talks.

Ajit Doval remains one of the key Indian officials responsible for carrying that process forward.
