Bengaluru Tried Restricting Vehicles on Flyovers. Now It Is Changing the Plan

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Thu, 10 Sep 2026 - 10:07
Verified

Bengaluru Traffic Police is changing its approach to vehicle restrictions on two major elevated corridors after a preliminary assessment of traffic conditions following the restrictions introduced earlier this month.

The decision comes just days after Bengaluru Traffic Police announced restrictions on specific categories of vehicles on major flyovers and elevated corridors from September 4.

We reported the original restrictions here: Bengaluru Traffic Police to Restrict Vehicles on 5 Major Flyovers From September 4, 2026

According to a Bengaluru Traffic Police press note titled “Phased Implementation of Traffic Restrictions on Major Flyovers,” the preliminary field assessment found that traffic movement on the BGS Flyover, Peenya Flyover and Double-Decker Flyover was smooth and efficient after the notified restrictions were implemented.

The situation was different on the International Airport Road Elevated Corridor, between Sanjeev Nagar Cross and Vidyashilpa Junction.

The press note says that diverting several categories of vehicles, including goods vehicles, two-wheelers and autorickshaws, at the approach ramps was resulting in traffic conflicts, queues and congestion at the diversion points. It also said the situation was affecting adjoining roads.

Because of this, the restrictions on the Airport Road elevated corridor are now being introduced in phases rather than imposing all the restrictions at once.

Goods vehicles will face restrictions first, while two-wheelers and autorickshaws will continue to be allowed for now.

The same phased approach is being applied to the Electronic City Elevated Corridor from September 11. Goods-vehicle restrictions will be implemented in the first phase, while two-wheelers will continue to be permitted.

This is an important change from the original plan.

The September 4 restrictions were introduced with the stated objectives of improving traffic movement and public safety. But the latest field assessment shows why traffic restrictions cannot be judged only by what happens on the flyover itself.

If vehicles are stopped from using an elevated road, they have to go somewhere else. If the alternative roads and approach points cannot handle the additional traffic, congestion can simply move from one location to another.

That appears to be what traffic police observed on the Airport Road corridor.

The latest decision therefore represents a shift from a blanket restriction to a phased approach based on observed traffic conditions.

For Bengaluru commuters, this means the rules on different flyovers may not be identical. The restrictions will depend on the corridor and the type of vehicle, and the press note says the remaining restrictions will be implemented in a calibrated manner after considering traffic flow, road safety, operational feasibility and the impact on adjoining roads.

There is also an interesting political angle to how the announcement reached the public. Karnataka Home Minister Priyank Kharge shared the Bengaluru Traffic Police press note on his official X account. As Home Minister, Kharge oversees the state's Home Department, under which the police administration operates. His sharing of the document therefore puts the traffic-police decision directly into the wider public and political conversation.

It does not, however, establish that Kharge personally issued the traffic restriction order. The document itself is a Bengaluru Traffic Police press note.

Bengaluru has tried many traffic solutions over the years. This time, the interesting part is that the authorities have already had an opportunity to observe what happened after the first restrictions were introduced — and are now changing the implementation where the initial approach created problems.
