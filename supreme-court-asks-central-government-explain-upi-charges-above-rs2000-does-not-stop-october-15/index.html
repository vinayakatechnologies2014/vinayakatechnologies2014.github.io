Supreme Court Asks Central Government to Explain UPI Charges Above ₹2,000, But Does Not Stop October 15 Rollout

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 29 Sep 2026 - 09:25
Developing

The Supreme Court on September 28, 2026, asked the Central Government to explain the legal and technical basis for the new UPI charges on certain merchant payments above ₹2,000.

However, the Court did not stop the October 15 rollout of the new framework.

A three-judge bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, along with Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana, also sought responses from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI).

The case was filed by advocate Anjan Datta, challenging the government notifications that introduced the new Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) framework.

The new system does not mean that everyone using UPI will have to pay a fee.

Under the framework, UPI payments between individuals remain free, regardless of the amount. Merchant payments of up to ₹2,000 remain free. A 0.4% MDR will apply to specified merchant transactions above ₹2,000. For transactions of ₹75,000 and above, the MDR is capped at ₹300 per transaction. Certain essential services will have a separate ₹5 flat MDR.

The Central Government has said the MDR is not a tax or a charge collected by the government. It is meant to be distributed among participants in the payment system, including banks and payment service providers. The government has also said around 96% of merchant UPI transactions will remain unaffected.

The Court wants the Central Government to explain what gives it the legal authority to introduce the new charge and how the money collected through MDR will work within the payment system.

The government, RBI and NPCI have been given four weeks to respond.

For now, the new framework is scheduled to take effect from October 15, 2026.

Buzz had earlier explained how UPI could remain free for users while selected merchant transactions would attract MDR, and why the new system does not mean that people will suddenly have to pay a fee every time they use UPI.

The Supreme Court's latest intervention now puts the legal basis of those new charges under examination.
