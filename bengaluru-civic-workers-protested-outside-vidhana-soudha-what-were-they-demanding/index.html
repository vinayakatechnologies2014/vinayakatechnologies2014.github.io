Bengaluru Civic Workers Protested Outside Vidhana Soudha. What Were They Demanding?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sun, 13 Sep 2026 - 11:22
Public Buzz

A group of Bengaluru civic sanitation workers and their supporters gathered outside Vidhana Soudha on September 10, protesting remarks made by Greater Bengaluru Development Minister Krishna Byre Gowda.

The protest was organised by the BBMP Pourakarmika Sangha, associated with the All India Central Council of Trade Unions (AICCTU). The workers’ immediate demand was an apology from the minister over his comments about sanitation workers and their demand for permanent employment.

The controversy began after Gowda questioned the demand to regularise civic workers, stating that around 30% of workers were not reporting for duty regularly. He said workers who repeatedly remained absent could face disciplinary action, including dismissal.

Worker representatives objected to what they described as collective blame and humiliation. They argued that disciplinary action should address individual cases of absenteeism rather than target the entire workforce.

The workers also raised concerns about:

  • Regularisation of contractual jobs
  • Timely payment of wages
  • ESI and PF benefits
  • Job security
  • Safety equipment
  • Working conditions
  • Alleged exploitation by contractors

The union also questioned why the minister had not held direct discussions with sanitation workers before making the remarks.

Police-action claims remain unverified

During the protest, there were claims that police took some protesters away from the area. However, the number of people taken away, the legal basis for the action and whether formal arrests were made have not been independently established.

Another allegation stated that police tore up a copy of the Preamble to the Constitution carried by one protester. This claim remains unverified and requires confirmation from the police or independent evidence.

The dispute comes as Bengaluru introduces changes to its civic administration and waste-management system. The government is seeking closer monitoring of waste collection, including GPS-based vehicle tracking and greater accountability for workers and contractors.

Workers, however, say that administrative efficiency must not come at the cost of their dignity, job security and labour rights.

The next question is whether the government will respond to the demand for an apology and address the workers’ wider concerns through a formal discussion.
