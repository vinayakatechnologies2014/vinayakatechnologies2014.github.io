Air India Appoints New CEO as Airline Begins Major Turnaround Effort

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 5 Aug 2026 - 18:05
Verified

Air India has appointed Tewolde Gebremariam as its new Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director, replacing Campbell Wilson, who is stepping down after leading the airline's transformation since its return to the Tata Group. Gebremariam, the former CEO of Ethiopian Airlines, takes charge at a critical stage of Air India's restructuring.

Gebremariam brings extensive experience from Ethiopian Airlines, where he led the carrier's expansion into one of Africa's major international airlines. His previous experience in managing major operational challenges is expected to be particularly relevant as Air India works through a difficult period.

The leadership change comes as Air India faces significant financial, operational and regulatory challenges. Reuters reported that the airline recorded a loss of more than $2 billion in the 2025–26 financial year, while the carrier is also dealing with regulatory scrutiny and the continuing impact of the June 2025 Air India crash.

Air India's turnaround is part of the Tata Group's broader effort to rebuild the airline into a major international carrier. Since the 2022 acquisition, the group has pursued fleet expansion, aircraft modernisation, technology upgrades, the Vistara merger and changes to customer service and operational systems.

Safety and regulatory compliance are likely to be among Gebremariam's most immediate priorities. The airline is operating under increased scrutiny following the 2025 crash and other reported compliance concerns, making operational discipline and safety standards central to the next phase of the turnaround.

Air India is also facing pressure from aircraft delivery delays, fleet modernisation requirements and geopolitical disruptions affecting international operations. These challenges come while the airline continues to work through the integration of its expanded group and a large aircraft order intended to support future growth.

Campbell Wilson's tenure saw Air India complete several major structural changes, including the merger of Vistara with Air India and significant investment in aircraft, technology and training infrastructure. Air India said Wilson would remain in the role until his successor was announced and in place, following his decision to step down in 2026.

The change in leadership therefore represents more than a routine CEO transition. Gebremariam is taking over at a point when Air India's next phase will depend on turning large-scale investment and restructuring into consistent operational performance, stronger safety standards and sustainable financial results.

For Air India, the challenge now is to prove that its transformation can move beyond fleet expansion and branding into a reliable, financially sustainable and globally competitive airline. The appointment of a CEO with experience in building and managing a major international carrier signals the Tata Group's intention to bring a different phase of leadership to that effort.
