Karnataka Gives Gun Amnesty Until November 1. Is the State Targeting Forest-Area Weapons First?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sun, 13 Sep 2026 - 07:17
Developing

Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar has asked people possessing unlicensed firearms to surrender them by November 1, 2026.

He warned that those who continue to possess unauthorised weapons after the deadline could face strict action. He also said that people living near forests who keep firearms to protect themselves or their livestock should either surrender unlicensed weapons or apply for the appropriate legal licence.

The announcement was made during a programme linked to National Forest Martyrs’ Day in Bengaluru.

The proposed drive appears to focus on:

  • Reducing the circulation of unlicensed firearms;
  • Preventing the misuse of country-made guns;
  • Improving security in and around forest areas;
  • Encouraging people with a genuine need for firearms to obtain legal licences.

However, the government has not yet published a detailed notification explaining how the surrender process will work.

It is not yet clear where weapons must be deposited, whether ammunition must also be surrendered, what receipt people will receive or what legal protection will apply.

The announcement comes against the background of the Hanur forest shooting in the Cauvery Wildlife Sanctuary in Chamarajanagar district.

In that incident, three forest personnel were accused of killing three people described as suspected poachers during a forest operation. The shooting took place in a remote forest area during the early hours.

The forest personnel maintained that the group had entered the protected forest carrying firearms and had opened fire first. They claimed that they acted in self-defence.

A fourth person, identified as Mahima Das, reportedly escaped from the area. Authorities registered a case against him. Two muzzle-loading guns and 38 spent cartridges were also reported to have been recovered in connection with the incident.

The Hanur incident highlights the risks associated with firearms in forest regions. Forest personnel may face armed groups or alleged poachers, while residents near forests may also keep weapons because of concerns about wild animals, livestock or personal safety.

At the same time, the circumstances of the shooting must be established through the investigation. The allegations that the deceased were poachers, that they fired first and that the forest personnel acted lawfully in self-defence should not be treated as final findings.

Shivakumar also referred to an earlier firearm-surrender effort in his home taluk of Kanakapura.

Why is the government acting now?

The available information points to several concerns:

  • Unlicensed firearms in forest and rural areas;
  • The safety of forest personnel;
  • Alleged poaching and illegal entry into protected forests;
  • Weapons kept for protection against wild animals;
  • The circulation of country-made guns;
  • The need to distinguish between illegal possession and people seeking lawful firearm licences.

It would be premature to conclude that the November 1 deadline was introduced solely because of the Hanur shooting. The Chief Minister’s remarks suggest a wider effort to address unlicensed firearm possession, with forest-area security forming an important part of the background.

The key question now is whether the Karnataka government will publish a clear procedure before the deadline.

Without a formal notification, people may not know where to surrender weapons, what legal protection they will receive or how to apply for a licence where a legitimate need exists.

For now, the announcement should be treated as a developing surrender drive, rather than a fully defined amnesty programme.
