Fact Check: Was India Granted Independence on a 99-Year Lease? The Viral Claim Explained

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Sun, 30 Aug 2026 - 14:04
Fact Checked

A viral claim circulating on social media suggests that India was not given complete independence from British rule in 1947, but was instead granted independence for 99 years. According to the claim, India's independence would therefore expire in 2046.

The claim is FALSE.

There is no provision in the Indian Independence Act, 1947 stating that India's independence was granted for 99 years. The viral claim appears to be based on a fabricated document that has been circulating online, but the actual legal and historical records of India's independence do not support the idea of a 99-year lease.

What Does the Indian Independence Act, 1947 Say?

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 was passed by the British Parliament on July 18, 1947. It provided the legal framework for the transfer of power and the creation of two independent Dominions — India and Pakistan.

The Act states that, from August 15, 1947, two independent Dominions would be established in India.

Importantly, there is no clause in the Act stating that India's independence would last for 99 years, nor does it contain a provision saying that India's sovereignty would return to Britain after 99 years.

The 99-year figure therefore has no basis in the legislation that formally provided for India's independence.

So What Actually Happened on August 15, 1947?

India became independent from British rule on August 15, 1947.

At the time, India became an independent Dominion, rather than immediately becoming a republic. This meant that some constitutional arrangements connected to the British Crown continued temporarily during the transition.

The Governor-General represented the Crown, but this did not mean that Britain continued to own India or that Indian independence had an expiry date.

The Constituent Assembly assumed the powers of the legislature of independent India and continued working on a permanent constitutional framework for the country.

The period immediately following independence was also marked by the Partition of British India into India and Pakistan, resulting in enormous displacement, violence and the movement of millions of people across the new borders.

At the same time, India's Constituent Assembly continued working on a permanent constitutional framework for the country.

What Changed in 1950?

India's transition from an independent Dominion to a republic took place when the Constitution of India came into force on January 26, 1950.

The Constitution had been adopted by the Constituent Assembly on November 26, 1949.

With the Constitution coming into force, India became a sovereign democratic republic, with its own constitutional system governing the country.

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 was subsequently repealed through Article 395 of the Constitution of India.

This is another important reason why the idea of a 99-year lease does not fit the historical record. India's constitutional development moved from independence in 1947 to the establishment of the Republic in 1950 — there was no provision setting an expiry date for Indian independence.

What the Evidence Shows

The claim that India was granted independence on a 99-year lease is FALSE.

The Indian Independence Act, 1947 established India as an independent Dominion from August 15, 1947. However, the Act contains no provision limiting India's independence to 99 years, and there is no legal basis for the claim that India's independence would expire in 2046.

The viral letter being shared as evidence of a 99-year arrangement is not supported by the official historical record. There is no credible historical or archival evidence of an agreement under which India was granted independence for a fixed period of 99 years.

India became independent on August 15, 1947, and remained an independent Dominion during the constitutional transition. When the Constitution of India came into force on January 26, 1950, India became a republic.

The historical and legal record therefore provides no evidence that India's independence was granted on a 99-year lease.
