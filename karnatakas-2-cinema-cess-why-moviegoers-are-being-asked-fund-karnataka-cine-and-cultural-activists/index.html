Karnataka’s 2% Cinema Cess: Why Moviegoers Are Being Asked to Fund Karnataka Cine and Cultural Activists Welfare — and What the High Court Said

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 22 Sep 2026 - 17:10
Developing

Going to a movie in Karnataka could soon cost a little more. The State government has introduced a 2% welfare cess on cinema tickets, with the money meant to fund welfare and social-security schemes for registered cine and cultural workers. But there is a simple question:

Why should a person buying a movie ticket pay an additional amount for the welfare of people working in the film and cultural industry?

The Karnataka Cine and Cultural Activists (Welfare) Act, 2024 was created to provide social security to people working in cinema and cultural activities. It creates a Welfare Board and a Social Security and Welfare Fund. The beneficiaries are not just actors or filmmakers. The law covers various skilled, unskilled, technical, artistic, manual and other workers connected with cinema and cultural activities.

The Act allows the government to impose a 1% to 2% welfare cess on cinema tickets and certain other cinema-related revenues. The 2025 Rules provide for the 2% ticket cess. The money is intended to go into the welfare fund and be used for schemes for eligible registered workers.

So, in simple terms:

Movie ticket → 2% cess → Welfare Fund → Registered cine and cultural workers

For example, on a ₹300 ticket, a 2% cess would amount to ₹6.

But not every person who has ever worked in a film automatically becomes a beneficiary. Workers have to qualify under the Act and Rules and register with the Welfare Board. The system provides for verification and a unique identity card. So, for example, a character artist who acted in one film would not automatically receive benefits simply because they appeared in that film.

The controversy is mainly about who should pay for this welfare system.

Instead of collecting a contribution from the profits of successful films or directly from the film industry, the law uses cinema-related transactions as one of the funding sources. That means the additional charge is ultimately connected to the cost of watching a movie.

The issue reached the Karnataka High Court after the government attempted to enforce the 2% cess from September 1, 2026. The Court quashed the enforcement measures because the 2024 Act had not yet been formally brought into force through the required government notification.

Importantly, the High Court did not permanently strike down the welfare scheme or declare that such a cess can never be imposed.

The Karnataka government has subsequently notified September 30, 2026 as the date on which the 2024 Act will come into force.

The Multiplex Association of India has meanwhile asked the government to reconsider the 2% ticket cess, arguing that it increases the cost for cinema-goers.

This leaves the larger question open:

Should welfare for cinema and cultural workers be funded through an additional charge on moviegoers, or should the film industry and the government contribute more directly?

Karnataka has chosen the first model by making cinema-related revenue one of the funding sources. The debate now is about who should ultimately bear the cost of that welfare system.
