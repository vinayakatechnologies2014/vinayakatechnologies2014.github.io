EPFO Just Changed the ₹15,000 Rule. Here’s What Employees Need to Know

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Thu, 17 Sep 2026 - 09:46
Verified

The Union Cabinet has approved a major change to the EPFO wage ceiling, raising it from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month. The change is expected to bring more than 51 lakh additional employees under mandatory EPFO coverage.

The new ceiling is meant to bring more workers into the formal social-security system as wages and incomes have increased over the years. The ₹15,000 ceiling was last revised in September 2014.

For employees earning between ₹15,000 and ₹25,000 a month, this could be a significant change. Under the earlier ceiling, a person joining a job with wages above ₹15,000 was not automatically covered under the mandatory EPF framework. With the ceiling now raised to ₹25,000, more employees in this salary range will come within mandatory EPFO coverage, subject to the applicable scheme provisions.

This means wider access to EPF savings, pension protection under the Employees’ Pension Scheme (EPS), and insurance protection under the Employees’ Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme (EDLI).

The change is not about giving employees an additional ₹10,000 in salary. The ₹25,000 figure is the wage ceiling used for determining mandatory coverage. The actual contribution and how it affects an employee's salary will depend on the applicable EPFO rules and the employee's eligible wages.

The government says the decision reflects sustained wage growth, rising incomes and the continued expansion of formal employment since the previous revision. In several states and occupations, minimum wages have also moved closer to the existing ₹15,000 threshold.

The government estimates an annual expenditure of around ₹11,339 crore, compared with existing annual budgetary support of about ₹10,250 crore. Over five years, the estimated expenditure is approximately ₹56,696 crore.

For workers, the immediate significance is simple: the salary threshold that has remained unchanged for more than a decade is being increased by ₹10,000.

Someone earning ₹18,000, ₹20,000 or ₹24,000 a month could now fall within the expanded mandatory coverage, whereas such employees could previously remain outside mandatory EPFO coverage depending on their circumstances.

The change is also relevant beyond monthly PF savings. EPFO operates three major social-security schemes — EPF, EPS and EDLI — covering retirement savings, pension and insurance protection.

The Ministry of Labour & Employment and EPFO will now undertake the necessary statutory and administrative steps for implementing the decision.

For employees, the key number to remember is therefore no longer ₹15,000. The Union Cabinet has approved a new EPFO wage ceiling of ₹25,000 per month, potentially bringing millions of additional workers into the formal social-security system.
