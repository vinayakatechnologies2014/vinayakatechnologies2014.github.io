Bengaluru Traffic Police to Restrict Vehicles on 5 Major Flyovers From September 4, 2026

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 1 Sep 2026 - 12:08
Verified

Bengaluru Traffic Police has announced restrictions on specific categories of vehicles on five major flyovers and elevated corridors in the city. Four corridors will come under the new rules from September 4, 2026, while restrictions on the Electronics City Elevated Corridor will begin from September 11.

The flyovers are not being completely closed. The restrictions apply to specific vehicle categories and, depending on the corridor, either throughout the day or during specified hours.

The decision follows a Bengaluru Traffic Police review of accidents, vehicle breakdowns and traffic conditions on elevated corridors.

Joint Commissioner of Police (Traffic) Karthik Reddy said the police considered factors including road safety, flyover design and width, accident severity, congestion caused by vehicle breakdowns, traffic at entry and exit points, emergency traffic management, and the safety of pedestrians and two-wheeler riders.

According to Reddy, vehicle breakdowns, particularly those involving goods-carrying vehicles, are a recurring concern on the city's flyovers. The restrictions are being introduced on a trial basis, after which BTP will assess the results and decide on further action.

The Electronics City Elevated Corridor will have the widest restrictions. Two-wheelers, passenger autorickshaws and three-wheeler goods autos will not be allowed throughout the day, while heavy, medium and light commercial goods vehicles will be restricted between 6 AM and 10 PM.

The move has also raised concerns among some commuters, particularly two-wheeler users. The main question is whether shifting restricted vehicles from elevated roads to the roads below could increase congestion on alternative routes.

The restrictions are therefore likely to be closely watched once they take effect. BTP's trial period should provide an opportunity to assess whether the measures improve road safety and traffic movement without creating additional pressure on surrounding roads.

For commuters, the important change is to check whether their vehicle falls under the restricted categories before using one of these corridors from the respective implementation date.
