Heavy Rain Expected Across Several States as Monsoon Activity Intensifies

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 11 Aug 2026 - 18:06
Developing

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has forecast widespread rainfall across several parts of India as active monsoon conditions continue. Its August 11 bulletin warned of heavy to very heavy rainfall in isolated places across parts of northern, central, eastern and western India over the coming days.

The weather system was particularly active over northwest India, where IMD forecast widespread rainfall over Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh and Uttarakhand during August 11–17. Heavy rainfall was also expected in several areas, with very heavy rainfall forecast over Haryana, Chandigarh and Delhi, Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab and West Uttar Pradesh on August 11.

East Rajasthan was among the areas facing a particularly strong rainfall threat. IMD reported that exceptionally heavy rainfall had already been recorded in parts of East Rajasthan in the 24 hours ending the morning of August 11, while heavy to very heavy rainfall was expected to continue in the region.

Central and eastern India were also expected to remain active. IMD forecast widespread rainfall over West Madhya Pradesh, East Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, while Odisha, Gangetic West Bengal, Jharkhand and other eastern regions were expected to receive widespread rainfall during different periods of the week.

In the northeast, rainfall activity was also expected to continue. IMD forecast widespread rainfall over Arunachal Pradesh and Assam & Meghalaya from August 12–17, with heavy rainfall possible in several northeastern states during the period.

Western parts of the country were also expected to see significant rainfall activity. Konkan and Goa, Madhya Maharashtra and Gujarat were among the regions identified in the forecast, with isolated heavy rainfall expected in parts of these areas.

For Karnataka, the August 11 IMD forecast indicated isolated to scattered rainfall in North and South Interior Karnataka from August 12–17 rather than placing the state among the main areas of heavy-to-very-heavy rainfall in that particular forecast. Coastal and interior Karnataka could nevertheless continue to experience changing monsoon conditions, making local forecasts important.

The rainfall pattern is being influenced by an active monsoon trough and developing weather systems, including a low-pressure area expected to form over the northwest Bay of Bengal and neighbouring areas around August 12. IMD said this system could contribute to further rainfall over Odisha and surrounding regions.

For residents, the most important point is that rainfall forecasts can change from one update to another. Heavy rain can also create localised flooding, waterlogging, reduced visibility, traffic disruption and difficult travel conditions, particularly in areas that receive intense rainfall over a short period.
