The reserves increased by $14.136 billion during the week, taking India's total reserve holdings above the $700 billion mark.

What Are Foreign-Exchange Reserves?

Foreign-exchange reserves are assets held by a country's central bank in foreign currencies and other reserve assets. They provide a financial buffer that can help a country manage external payments and periods of pressure on its currency.

For India, foreign-exchange reserves comprise foreign-currency assets, gold, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs), and India's Reserve Tranche Position with the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

How Much Does India Have?

As of August 7, India's total foreign-exchange reserves stood at $707.002 billion.

Foreign-currency assets accounted for approximately $574.6 billion, while gold reserves were valued at about $108.7 billion.

SDRs stood at approximately $18.7 billion, while India's Reserve Tranche Position with the IMF was around $4.9 billion.

The Role of NRI Deposits

Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank), or FCNR(B), deposits are foreign-currency deposits maintained by eligible non-resident Indians with Indian banks.

The RBI has introduced measures relating to FCNR(B) deposits and foreign-exchange swaps as part of its efforts to support foreign-currency liquidity.

These measures are relevant because foreign-currency inflows can contribute to India's overall external financial position.

What Does This Mean for the Rupee?

Large foreign-exchange reserves give the RBI a substantial financial cushion when the currency comes under pressure.

However, high reserves do not automatically mean that the rupee will strengthen.

The value of the rupee is influenced by several factors, including crude oil prices, dollar demand, foreign investment, trade flows, global financial conditions and movements in the foreign-exchange market.

For India, maintaining adequate foreign-exchange reserves is an important part of managing external-sector risks.

What Does It Mean for Ordinary Indians?

For most people, the $707 billion figure will not immediately change anything in their daily lives.

Its importance is more indirect. A strong reserve position can give the RBI greater room to respond to currency volatility, which can eventually affect the cost of imported products, overseas education, international travel, fuel and other goods influenced by the rupee-dollar exchange rate.

Does It Mean Petrol Prices Will Fall?

Not necessarily.

India imports a large amount of its crude oil, so domestic fuel prices are affected by international crude prices, the rupee-dollar exchange rate, taxes and other factors.

A rise in foreign-exchange reserves by itself does not guarantee cheaper petrol or diesel.
