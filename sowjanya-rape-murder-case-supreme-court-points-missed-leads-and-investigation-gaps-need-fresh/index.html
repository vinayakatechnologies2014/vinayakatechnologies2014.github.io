Sowjanya Rape-Murder Case: Supreme Court Points to Missed Leads and Investigation Gaps That Need Fresh Examination

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Tue, 22 Sep 2026 - 13:43
Verified

The Supreme Court's detailed judgment in the 2012 Sowjanya rape-murder case, Kusumavati Gowda vs. The Government of Karnataka (Criminal Appeal No. 4477 of 2026, arising from SLP (Crl.) No. 5763 of 2026; 2026 INSC 1030), was delivered on September 21, 2026.

In the judgment, the Supreme Court explains why it has ordered a completely fresh investigation and highlights the missed leads, investigative gaps and evidence that still need to be examined.

Here are the key points the Supreme Court highlighted:

1. Belthangady Police, Karnataka CID and CBI investigated the case, but the prosecution ultimately failed to establish who committed the crime. The case was first investigated by Belthangady Police, later transferred to the Karnataka CID in December 2012, and then handed over to the CBI in November 2013. The CBI eventually prosecuted Santhosh Rao, but the Sessions Court acquitted him on June 16, 2023.

The Supreme Court questioned how these agencies conducted the investigation and how the available evidence was gathered and evaluated.

2. Eight potential witnesses identified by Sowjanya’s family were located within three weeks after the Supreme Court ordered their protection. On April 29, 2026, the Supreme Court directed Karnataka to protect eight potential witnesses mentioned by the petitioner. The Karnataka authorities located them within three weeks.

The Supreme Court said this raised questions about why the earlier investigating agencies had not traced or examined these leads when they were available much closer to the time of the crime.

3. The Court found that potentially useful forensic evidence is still available. DNA expert PW-18 Vinod J. Lakkappan told the Supreme Court that hair from another person was found on Material Object No. 15 — Sowjanya’s chudidar top. He also said that if relevant persons are identified, their blood samples can still be collected and compared. This was important because the Karnataka High Court, on August 30, 2024, had rejected the request for reinvestigation, partly observing that important evidence that should have been collected during the early “golden hour” might no longer be available.

The Supreme Court held that the passage of 14 years does not by itself make a fresh investigation pointless.

4. The Supreme Court strongly criticised the investigation carried out by Belthangady Police, the Karnataka CID and the CBI. While examining how the three agencies handled the case, the Supreme Court said that “the manner in which the investigation had progressed and the manner in which the various investigating agencies conducted the investigation and gathered and evaluated the evidence shocks the very conscience of the court.”

The Supreme Court said the investigation had caused injustice to the victim, who was 17 years old when she was raped and murdered.

5. The Supreme Court said the possibility that the real culprit or culprits manipulated the investigation cannot be ruled out. The Supreme Court said that, considering the way the investigation progressed, the possibility of manipulation by the actual perpetrator or perpetrators could not be ruled out. However, the Supreme Court did not say that any particular person manipulated the investigation.

The Supreme Court said it was expressing no prima facie opinion about the role of Mallik Jain, Deeraj Jain or Uday Jain, and their role, if any, has to be determined through the fresh investigation.

6. The new SIT must investigate the case independently and cannot include officers involved in the earlier investigations. The Supreme Court directed the Karnataka government to constitute a Special Investigation Team (SIT) headed by a senior police officer.

The Supreme Court directed, The new SIT cannot include any officer who was involved in the earlier investigation by Belthangady Police, the Karnataka CID or the CBI.

7. Santhosh Rao cannot be investigated or prosecuted again for the same offence. Rao was tried and acquitted by the Sessions Court on June 16, 2023. The Supreme Court has specifically protected him from being subjected to further investigation or prosecution for the same offence, relying on Article 20(2) of the Constitution and Section 300 of the CrPC.

Therefore, Santhosh Rao cannot be investigated or prosecuted again for the same offence.

8. The new SIT has three months to complete the fresh investigation and file its report. The Supreme Court has ordered that the de novo investigation must be completed within three months, and the SIT must then file its police report before the concerned court. The matter will thereafter proceed according to law.

Official Source: Kusumavati Gowda vs The Government Of Karnataka on 21 September, 2026
