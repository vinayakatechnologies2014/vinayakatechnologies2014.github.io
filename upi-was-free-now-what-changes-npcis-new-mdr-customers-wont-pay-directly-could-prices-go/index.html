UPI Was Free — Now What Changes? NPCI’s New MDR: Customers Won’t Pay Directly, But Could Prices Go Up?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 16 Sep 2026 - 08:45
Developing

UPI is not becoming a paid service for everyone. The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has introduced a new Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) framework for selected UPI merchant transactions. The new framework will take effect from October 15, 2026.

The Government of India has clarified the main points around the new system:

  1. The new MDR applies only to selected P2M UPI transactions above ₹2,000.
  2. The standard MDR is 0.4%, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 and above.
  3. P2P transactions remain completely free, regardless of the amount transferred.
  4. UPI payments to merchants up to ₹2,000 remain free.
  5. The government says approximately 96% of P2M transactions will remain unaffected.
  6. UPI apps will not be allowed to impose a separate platform fee or other charge on customers for UPI payments, according to the government.
  7. The MDR is not a government tax. The government says the revenue will be distributed among participants in the payment ecosystem.
  8. The government says the revenue will support infrastructure resilience, cybersecurity, fraud prevention, innovation and customer service.
  9. The framework includes protections for small merchants under the zero-MDR arrangements.
  10. Certain sectors have special MDR treatment rather than the standard 0.4%.
  11. The new framework starts October 15, 2026.

For customers, this means there is no new 0.4% fee that they are expected to pay whenever they use UPI. For example, on an eligible ₹3,000 merchant payment, the MDR would be ₹12, but the customer is not supposed to be asked to pay that ₹12 separately.

The direct impact is therefore on businesses and other participants in the payment ecosystem handling eligible transactions above ₹2,000. The effect will vary depending on the merchant category and whether the business falls under an exemption.

The bigger question is what businesses will do with the additional cost. The government has said merchants should not pass the MDR directly to customers. However, businesses could still review their overall pricing or payment practices to account for their costs. Whether that results in higher prices or other changes is something that can only be assessed after the new system starts.

This also raises a question about lower-income customers. The MDR itself is not a direct charge on them, and many everyday UPI payments will remain outside the framework. But if businesses eventually increase prices to compensate for payment costs, even small increases could matter more to households with tighter budgets. That is a possible indirect effect, not something established by the announcement.

The new framework also adds an important update to Buzz's earlier article, Rumor: Will People in India Have to Pay to Use UPI? Here's What the Government's Latest Move Means. The latest development does not introduce a blanket fee for using UPI. Instead, it creates an MDR structure for selected higher-value merchant transactions while keeping P2P transfers and most merchant transactions outside the new charge.

The real question to watch after October 15 is therefore not simply whether UPI users will be charged, but whether businesses absorb the new MDR or whether consumers eventually notice its effects through prices or changes in how larger UPI payments are handled.

Official sources: Government of India — Press Information Bureau
