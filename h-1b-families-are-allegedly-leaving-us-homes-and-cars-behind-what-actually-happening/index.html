H-1B Families Are Allegedly Leaving US Homes and Cars Behind. What Is Actually Happening?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Fri, 11 Sep 2026 - 10:01
Rumor

A viral claim circulating online says some H-1B families who recently lost their jobs in the United States are returning to India without selling their homes, cars and other belongings.

The claim reportedly involves more than 10 families in the Celina, Melissa and surrounding Dallas areas. According to the social-media post, some families were unable to find new jobs and could no longer afford their mortgages or other expenses, leading them to leave the US rather than wait for a sale. However, there is currently no verified evidence showing that this is a widespread trend. The number of families cited in the viral claim is also too small to establish a broader pattern.

That does not mean every individual case is necessarily false. It means the larger conclusion — that H-1B families are broadly abandoning American homes and cars and returning to India — has not been independently established.

But the timing of the claim is significant.

On September 10, the US Department of Homeland Security proposed eliminating the existing up-to-60-day grace period available to certain nonimmigrant workers, including H-1B holders, after their employment ends. Under the current system, eligible workers can use that period to look for another employer, change status or prepare to leave the country.

The proposed change would remove that discretionary grace period and restore the previous approach, under which a worker's immigration status is more directly tied to the employment or activity that formed the basis of the visa. The proposal is not yet a final rule.

That creates a very different situation for someone who has a family, a mortgage, a car and years of belongings in the US. The existing 60-day period was specifically intended to give affected workers time to find another job, change status or make preparations to leave.

So the viral story may be pointing to a real problem faced by some individuals, but it is too early to call it a mass H-1B exodus.

The bigger question is whether more such cases could emerge if layoffs continue while the immigration rules become less forgiving. If the proposed DHS rule eventually takes effect, workers who lose their jobs could have considerably less time to reorganise their finances, employment and family arrangements.
