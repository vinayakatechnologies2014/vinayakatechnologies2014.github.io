Yash Faces Backlash Over Dharmasthala Visit as Sowjanya Activists Call for Toxic Boycott

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 26 Aug 2026 - 11:21
Public Buzz

Kannada superstar Yash is facing criticism from supporters seeking justice for Sowjanya over his meetings with Dharmasthala Manjunatha Swamy Temple authorities and other influential people associated with Dharmasthala on Tuesday.

Activists have clarified that their objection is not to Yash visiting the temple or offering prayers, but to his decision to meet temple authorities and other influential people associated with Dharmasthala at a time when the Sowjanya case remains under public and legal scrutiny.

Yash visited the temple along with Toxic producer K. Venkat Narayana, ahead of the film's release on August 26. The controversy comes as the 2012 Sowjanya rape and murder case is before the Supreme Court, with her family seeking a fresh investigation.

Why Are Activists Angry With Yash?

Sowjanya activist Girish Mattannavar said there was nothing wrong with Yash visiting the temple as a devotee. According to him, the objection is to the people the actor chose to meet during the visit.

Mattannavar said Yash's public standing means that such interactions can be interpreted as more than a personal meeting, especially when allegations surrounding Dharmasthala continue to be discussed and the legal process concerning the Sowjanya case is still underway.

"He is at liberty to visit the Manjunatha shrine and pray for his well-being. However, meeting influential persons who are facing accusations at a time when investigations are underway is wrong. He is sending a wrong message to his fans", Mattannavar said.

He also questioned why Yash had not visited Sowjanya's mother or publicly expressed support for her family's long search for justice.

The criticism has also found resonance among some members of the Vokkaliga community, to which Sowjanya and her family belonged. Critics have questioned whether a prominent public figure from Karnataka should have shown greater sensitivity towards the family while meeting people associated with the Dharmasthala controversy.

Why Is the Sowjanya Case Back Before the Supreme Court?

The case dates back to October 2012, when 17-year-old Sowjanya was raped and murdered in Dharmasthala.

After the initial investigation, the case was investigated by the Karnataka CID and was later handed over to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). The CBI prosecuted Santhosh Rao in the case.

However, in June 2023, a special CBI court acquitted Rao after finding that the prosecution had failed to establish the case against him with sufficient evidence. The acquittal led Sowjanya's family and supporters to renew their demand for a fresh investigation.

Sowjanya's mother, Kusumavathi Gowda, subsequently approached the Karnataka High Court seeking reinvestigation. The High Court declined to order a fresh probe in 2024. Her family then approached the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court is now considering whether the circumstances of the case justify a fresh investigation.

What Did the Supreme Court Question?

During hearings in July 2026, the Supreme Court raised concerns about several aspects of the earlier investigation.

The Bench questioned whether the CBI had conducted a sufficiently independent investigation and examined issues relating to how evidence and witnesses had been handled. The Court was also told about investigative steps that, according to the petitioner's side, had either not been taken or had not been adequately pursued.

The Court's questioning became particularly sharp when the CBI opposed the request for a fresh investigation. During the hearing, the Bench asked the pointed question, "Who are you trying to protect?"

The question was made in the context of the Court examining why the CBI was resisting another investigation when the person it had prosecuted had already been acquitted.

The Court has reserved its order on the request for reinvestigation. This means that no final decision directing a fresh probe had been issued at the time of the latest proceedings. The Court has also directed that witness protection should continue while it considers the matter.

The legal position is therefore important: the Supreme Court's questions and criticism of the investigation do not establish the guilt of any person. The Court is examining whether the case requires another investigation and what course of action should follow.

Calls to Boycott Toxic

The criticism of Yash has now extended to his new film Toxic.

Some Sowjanya supporters have called on fans to stay away from the film, describing the proposed boycott as a way of expressing solidarity with Sowjanya's family.

For the activists, the issue is not Yash's religious faith or his decision to visit a temple. Their concern is that his meetings during the visit could be perceived as showing support or closeness to people whom they believe should remain under scrutiny until the various legal and investigative questions are settled.

The controversy comes just as Toxic reaches theatres, making Yash's Dharmasthala visit an unexpected part of the film's release-day conversation.

A Bigger Debate About Celebrities and Public Responsibility

Yash has a massive following in Karnataka and across India, and supporters of the actor may view the Dharmasthala visit simply as a personal religious journey.

His critics see it differently.

They argue that celebrities with millions of followers cannot always separate their public actions from the message those actions may send. For those campaigning for Sowjanya's family, the actor's decision to meet influential people at a time when the case is again receiving attention from the Supreme Court has therefore become a matter of public concern.

Whether the boycott calls will have any meaningful impact on Toxic remains uncertain.

For now, the controversy has placed Yash's Dharmasthala visit under intense scrutiny and revived a broader conversation in Karnataka about celebrity influence, public sensitivity and the long-running demand for justice for Sowjanya.
