Fact Check: Did the Government Really Announce a Nationwide School Holiday?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 12 Aug 2026 - 18:07
Fact Checked

A message circulating online claims that schools across India have been ordered to remain closed because of heavy rainfall. The claim is misleading: there is no single nationwide school-closure order covering all schools in India because of the monsoon situation.

Heavy rainfall has affected several parts of the country, and the India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued weather warnings for different regions. However, weather warnings and school closures are separate decisions, with state and local authorities determining whether schools should remain open, close temporarily or change their schedules.

Recent school-closure information demonstrates this difference. On August 11, 2026, schools in some parts of Uttarakhand, including Haridwar and Bageshwar, were reported closed because of rain, while other states were following different arrangements based on local conditions. There was no nationwide closure.

The situation continued to vary on August 13. Reports indicated school closures or changes in several areas because of heavy rainfall warnings and local circumstances, while other parts of the country continued with normal schedules. This means that a closure announced for one district or state cannot automatically be applied to students across India.

The IMD itself operates a district-level warning system rather than issuing a blanket school-closure order. The government has stated that IMD prepares and updates district-wise weather warnings several times a day, while warning communication and local emergency responses involve state and district authorities.

This distinction is important because an IMD red, orange or yellow weather warning does not automatically mean that every school in the affected region is closed. Authorities may consider rainfall intensity, flooding, landslides, road conditions, local safety risks and the specific location of schools before making a closure decision.

The Ministry of Home Affairs has also confirmed that the Centre monitors monsoon conditions and coordinates with agencies including IMD, the Central Water Commission and state and district emergency authorities. Its July 2026 updates referred to heavy rains and related impacts in several states, but this monitoring role does not amount to a nationwide order directing all schools to close.

For parents and students, the safest approach is therefore to check the announcement issued by the relevant state education department, district administration or individual school. A social-media graphic claiming that “all schools in India are closed” should not be treated as an official notification unless it can be traced to an appropriate government authority.

The same applies to messages that list several states together and describe them as being under a single nationwide holiday order. A school holiday in one district, city or state does not automatically create a holiday elsewhere in India.

There is no evidence of a blanket nationwide school-closure order based on the current monsoon situation. School closures have been announced in specific states, districts or local areas, depending on weather conditions and decisions by the relevant authorities.

What is true: Heavy rainfall and weather warnings have affected several parts of India, and some local authorities have closed schools for safety reasons.

What is not true: These local closures should not be presented as a nationwide school holiday announced by the Government of India.
