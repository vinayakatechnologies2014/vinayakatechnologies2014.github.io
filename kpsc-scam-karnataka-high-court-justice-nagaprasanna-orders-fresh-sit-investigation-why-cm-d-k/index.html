KPSC Scam: Karnataka High Court Justice Nagaprasanna Orders Fresh SIT Investigation — Why Is CM D K Shivakumar Objecting?

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Thu, 24 Sep 2026 - 09:37
Developing

The Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC) recruitment controversy has taken a new turn after the Karnataka High Court ordered a fresh investigation through a Special Investigation Team (SIT) into alleged irregularities in the recruitment of 400 Veterinary Officers.

The order was passed on September 21, 2026, by Justice M. Nagaprasanna of the Karnataka High Court.

The court constituted a five-member SIT headed by senior IPS officer Alok Kumar. Divya V. Gopinath, Anup A. Shetty, Kushal Chouksey and Kshama Mishra are the other members.

The High Court directed the existing CID and jurisdictional police to hand over their investigation records, documents and electronic evidence to the SIT. The new team has been given 100 days to complete the investigation, with progress reports to be submitted to the court every 30 days.

The court also made it clear that the SIT should follow the evidence wherever it leads, including investigating other persons, offences or transactions that emerge during the investigation.

This follows our earlier report, KPSC Scam: Court Calls It “Systematic Fraud” — How Deep Does Karnataka’s Recruitment Network Go?, which examined the serious allegations surrounding the recruitment process and the court's concerns over the functioning of the recruitment system.

The latest order takes the matter further. Instead of allowing the existing investigation to continue, the High Court chose a fresh SIT instead of transferring the case to the CBI. Justice Nagaprasanna recorded that, considering the circumstances, continuation of the investigation by the CID or jurisdictional police was not appropriate, while a CBI investigation was not considered the only alternative.

The judicial process is therefore clear: the High Court has ordered a fresh investigation and put a specific team in charge of it.

The controversy has come from the State government.

Chief Minister D K Shivakumar has questioned the High Court's decision to name the officers for the SIT, arguing that selection of investigating officers should be left to the government. The Karnataka government has decided to challenge the High Court's order before the Supreme Court.

That objection becomes interesting when viewed alongside another statement made by Shivakumar.

At around the same time, the Chief Minister said the government was examining whether KPSC itself could be shut down within the legal framework, citing repeated recruitment controversies.

That raises a straightforward question:

If the Karnataka Government is concerned enough about KPSC to consider the future of the commission itself, why should a court-ordered investigation into alleged irregularities become a point of confrontation?

The High Court has not convicted anyone through its order. It has ordered an investigation into allegations that affect the credibility of public recruitment.

For KPSC candidates, that credibility is the real issue.

Government jobs are supposed to be filled through a process where candidates compete on equal terms and selection is based on merit. If there are allegations of manipulation, question-paper leaks, favouritism or other irregularities, those allegations need to be properly investigated and either established or rejected on evidence.

Justice Nagaprasanna's order has created a mechanism for doing exactly that: a new team, a defined mandate, a deadline and judicial monitoring.

The government has the constitutional right to challenge the order before the Supreme Court. But until the order is stayed or changed by a higher court, the SIT constituted by the Karnataka High Court remains responsible for the fresh investigation.

The bigger question for Karnataka people is therefore simple. If the government believes KPSC has serious problems, the priority should be to find out what went wrong, who was responsible and how the recruitment system can be made trustworthy again.

The KPSC controversy should not become another fight between institutions.
