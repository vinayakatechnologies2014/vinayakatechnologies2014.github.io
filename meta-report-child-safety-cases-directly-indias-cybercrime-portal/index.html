Meta to Report Child-Safety Cases Directly to India’s Cybercrime Portal

Submitted by Karthik Kumar D K on Wed, 16 Sep 2026 - 10:08
Developing

Meta, the parent company of Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, will now report child-safety matters directly to India’s Cybercrime Reporting Portal managed by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C).

The decision was confirmed by a Meta spokesperson on September 15, 2026. Government sources also confirmed that Meta had agreed to report child-safety cases directly to Indian law-enforcement authorities. Meta said protecting children on its platforms is a priority and that it is committed to working with the government to hold perpetrators responsible.

The move is particularly relevant to cases involving child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and other forms of child sexual exploitation and abuse.

The change also modifies how relevant cases involving India are reported. In a July 7, 2026 post on its official Newsroom, Meta explained that when it became aware of apparent child exploitation, it reported such cases to law-enforcement authorities through the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC). For India, Meta said NCMEC reported the material to the national cybercrime reporting portal on Meta's behalf.

Under the new arrangement, Meta will report child-safety matters directly to the I4C-managed cybercrime portal, removing that intermediary step for the relevant reports.

India’s I4C, under the Ministry of Home Affairs, operates the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. The government portal has a dedicated facility for reporting cybercrime related to women and children, including complaints involving Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) and other sexually explicit content involving children.

The portal also allows certain complaints involving women and children to be reported anonymously, while complaints can be submitted and tracked through the system.

For investigators, the direct reporting arrangement could make it easier to receive relevant information from Meta without relying on the previous NCMEC reporting route. However, the announcement does not establish that every case will automatically be investigated faster. The actual response will depend on the information provided and how the relevant law-enforcement agencies handle each case.

The announcement comes after increased scrutiny of online child safety in India. In July, Meta itself acknowledged concerns over Instagram advertisements in India that violated its policies against child exploitation and said it had removed violating advertisements and accounts.

Meta's latest decision therefore represents a change in its reporting and cooperation process with Indian authorities, rather than the creation of a new complaint portal for ordinary users.

There are still some details that have not been publicly explained. These include:

  1. What exact categories of child-safety matters will be reported directly to I4C?
  2. Will all India-related cases covered by Meta's reporting obligations follow the new direct route?
  3. What information and evidence will accompany each report?
  4. What response process will apply to urgent cases?
  5. How will Indian agencies coordinate with international authorities when suspects, victims or servers are outside India?
  6. Will Meta publish India-specific figures showing how many reports are sent directly to I4C?

For users, there is no new reporting action required because of this change. The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal remains the government’s existing mechanism for people to report cybercrime involving women and children.

The significance of the announcement is mainly on the law-enforcement side: Meta is changing the way relevant child-safety information is passed to Indian authorities. How much difference the direct channel makes will become clearer as its implementation and reporting data emerge.
